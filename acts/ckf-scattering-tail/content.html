CKF scattering tail prototype

Four ways to let the CKF accept a hit after a non-Gaussian scatter, compared with the plain Gaussian CKF at a tight and at inflated chi2 cuts. ODD, ttbar with <μ> = 200, Fatras with the general mixture scattering model, %EVENTS% events.

Verdict

Do not stay with the inflated chi2 cut. At every fake rate, a tail treatment at the tight cut 15/25 finds more tail tracks than an inflated cut. It also fits them better, and it uses less CPU than the 49/100 cut.

The mixture is the most promising candidate overall. It reaches the tail efficiency of the 25/50 cut (93.7 %, against 90.4 % at 15/25) with no extra fakes (0.204 %). It is the only candidate that makes the pulls correct: the q/p pull q68 is 0.99 for the no-tail tracks and 1.34 for the tail tracks, against 1.12 and 1.59 for the baseline. The CPU cost is +18 % for the CKF.

The retry gets the most tail efficiency (95.2 %, close to the 95.8 % of 49/100) at a fake rate of 0.26 % instead of 0.69 %, for +14 % CKF time. It needs no API change, so it is the cheapest step to put into ACTS.

Retry + branch adds fakes and CPU for 0.15 points of efficiency. Select-only gives the worst tail pulls of all configurations, which shows that the update must open the covariance to capture the kink.

Setup

  • Chain as full_chain_odd.py --ttbar: Pythia8 ttbar with 200 pile-up, Fatras, smearing digitization, default triplet seeding, two-way CKF, greedy ambiguity resolution. Numbers are after the ambiguity resolution.
  • Fatras uses GeneralMixture scattering (Frühwirth–Liendl) instead of Highland. The KF keeps the Highland Gaussian. For thin silicon the truth core is 1.10× Highland, and 0.7–0.9 % of the crossings are beyond 4× Highland.
  • Truth tail flag: Fatras records every scatter with a projected angle above 3× Highland. A primary is a tail primary if at least one scatter is above %RATIO%× Highland at ρ > %RHOMIN% mm, which is inside the tracker.
  • Efficiency: primaries with pT > 1 GeV, |η| < 3, ≥ 9 measurements, matched by TrackTruthMatcher. Fake rate: tracks classified as fake. Resolution: matched tracks, q68 is the 68 % quantile of |residual|.
  • CPU: TrackFindingAlgorithm time per event, single thread, 20 events per run, configurations interleaved and repeated. Same machine, RelWithDebInfo with forced assertions.

Tail model

The reconstruction needs a tail weight ε and a tail width k for each crossing. A two-Gaussian fit (EM, 2M samples) to the Fatras semi-Gaussian model of one projected angle gives both as a function of x/X0. The CKF interpolates this table in ln(x/X0). The model uses Z = 14 and β = 1 for every material.

The two-Gaussian fit reproduces the fraction beyond 4× Highland to within 10 %. The Highland Gaussian that the KF uses has almost nothing there.

k is in units of the Highland width, so the tail variance is k² times the KF scattering variance.

Implementation

  • detail::ScatteringTailState in the CKF result holds Σ εi Di and Σ εi since the last accepted measurement. Each material interaction adds Di = (k² − 1) Qi. The tail covariance is the ε-weighted mean D = Σ εi Di / Σ εi, because one scatterer is assumed to be in the tail.
  • The state is transported with the segment jacobian that filter() already has: one 6×6 sandwich per surface. It is reset after each accepted measurement and when a branch restarts.
  • A gate skips the tail when D adds less than 5 % to the predicted loc0 and loc1 variance.
  • Retry: if the core selection gives a hole or an outlier, call the track state creator again with C + D. The update then uses C + D.
  • Retry + branch: as retry, but the hole or outlier stays as a second branch.
  • Select only: as retry, but the update uses C. This is the "inflate only the selection" option.
  • Mixture: one selection with χ²mix = −2 ln[(1−ε) N(r; Sc) + ε N(r; St)], shifted so that it equals the core chi2 for a small residual. Each selected state gets the posterior covariance C + wt D, with the posterior tail weight wt. New delegates createTrackStatesWithTail and MeasurementSelector::selectWithTail.
  • Each state stores the chi2 that the selection used; the track chi2 stays the Kalman sum.

Efficiency

  • The tail efficiency of the 15/25 baseline falls to 88 % at 1 GeV, because there the scattering dominates the prediction error. All treatments recover most of this loss. Below 5 GeV the order is the same in every bin: retry + branch ≈ retry > select only > mixture > 15/25. Above 5 GeV all configurations agree within the errors.
  • The no-tail efficiency also rises by 0.2–0.3 points with every treatment. These particles have scatters just below the 4× threshold of the truth flag, or several medium scatters.
  • The retry has the most efficiency because it accepts any hit that passes 15/25 under C + D. The mixture also pays the prior penalty −2 ln[ε/(1−ε)] and the det term ln(det St/det Sc), so the tail must describe a hit clearly better than the core does.

Fake rate

  • The mixture keeps the fake rate of 15/25 (0.204 % against 0.208 %). Select only adds 0.01 points, retry 0.06, retry + branch 0.12. The inflated cut adds 0.03 (25/50), 0.16 (35/70) and 0.48 points (49/100).
  • The inflated cut adds fakes in every pT bin. The largest absolute rise is at 1–1.5 GeV (0.30 % to 1.03 % for 49/100). Above 5 GeV, where 15/25 has almost no fakes, 49/100 has about 0.4 %.

Resolution and pulls

  • An inflated cut accepts the tail hit but keeps the core covariance, so the fit must absorb the kink with the other parameters. The q/p pull q68 of tail tracks grows from 1.59 (15/25) to 1.69 (49/100). This is the bias the design note expected.
  • Retry and retry + branch update with C + D and lower the tail q/p pull q68 to 1.56, and the fraction beyond 5σ from 2.25 % to 1.62 %. Select only uses the same hits but updates with C, and gives 1.65 and 2.70 %, the worst of all.
  • The mixture gives 1.34 and 0.93 %. It also brings the no-tail q/p pull q68 from 1.12 to 0.99. The likely reason: the truth core is 1.10× Highland, so the plain KF is slightly too confident, and the small posterior tail weight on every hit adds the missing variance. This is not verified separately.
  • The d0 residual q68 of tail tracks is 64–65 µm with every treatment, against 67 µm for 15/25. The no-tail resolution does not change.

p-values

State p-value: the predicted chi2 that the selection used (mixture: χ²mix; select only: the chi2 with C + D), with ndf = measurement dimension (pixel 3 incl. time, short strip 2, long strip 1). The three innermost measurements of each track are left out, because the wide seed covariance absorbs them with a chi2 near 0. Track p-value: the Kalman chi2Sum with NDF − 6. The first measurements fix the 6 track parameters (time included) from the wide seed covariance and add almost no chi2, so NDF alone pushes the p-value towards 1.

  • The state p-value of the selection has an excess at low p for all configurations; it is the scattering tail. The inflated cuts extend it down to 1e-11, so 1.46 % of the non-seed states of 49/100 have p < 1e-3, against 0.21 % for 15/25.
  • The track p-value shows the cost of the inflated cut most clearly: 8.0 % of the 49/100 tracks have p < 1e-3, and 5.3 % of the 35/70 tracks, against 1.2 % for 15/25, 1.0 % for the mixture and 2.4 % for the retry. A later p-value cut on the CKF chi2 would remove the tail tracks again that the inflated cut found.
  • The mixture gives the best calibrated track chi2: chi2 / (NDF − 6) is 1.04 for matched tracks, and its track p-value is almost flat. 15/25 has 1.10 and a falling slope, because the KF core (Highland) is 10 % narrower than the truth core. 49/100 has 1.27 and a spike at p ≈ 0 from the accepted tail hits.
  • Per state, chi2/ndf is 1.00–1.03 for the mixture, 1.02–1.12 for 15/25 and 1.14–1.30 for 49/100 (states 4 and later from the inside). The three innermost states have 0.41: they absorb the track parameters.

CPU

  • Baseline 15/25: 2.13 s per event. Every looser setting costs CPU, because more branches survive and branches live longer. The inflated cut costs +7.8 % (25/50), +14.0 % (35/70) and +24.7 % (49/100).
  • Select only +12.4 %, retry +13.9 %, mixture +18.5 %, retry + branch +20.1 %. The repeated runs agree within 1.5 %; the 15/25 and 49/100 anchors of the two timing sessions agree within 1.5 %.
  • The tail bookkeeping itself is cheap: one 6×6 sandwich per surface. The likely main cost is the extra branches that the tail keeps alive; this is not profiled. The retry accepts 8 200 tail states per event, but the output has only 901 tracks per event (17 more than 15/25), so most tail states sit on branches that are dropped later.
  • The mixture prototype computes the core and the tail chi2 and two determinants for every candidate when the gate passes, and gives every selected state its own prediction. A lazy tail term (only when the core chi2 is large) should remove part of its extra 4.6 points over the retry. Not measured.

Conclusion

Stay with the inflated cut? No. On this sample the inflated cut is the worst way to buy tail efficiency. Each step from 15/25 to 49/100 adds fakes, makes the tail pulls worse and spoils the track p-value: 8.0 % of the 49/100 tracks have p < 1e-3, against 1.2 % for 15/25. It is also not cheap: 35/70 costs as much CPU as the retry, and 49/100 costs more than any treatment.

Caveats

  • The simulation and the reconstruction tail model come from the same Fatras model. Real material, other Z and low β change ε and k. The result shows what a correct tail model gives, not how robust it is to a wrong one.
  • One sample, one detector, one seeding. The statistical errors are small (tail efficiency ± 0.2 points), but all configurations share the events, so the differences are more significant than the errors suggest.
  • The CKF time includes the forced assertions of the dev3 build. The relative numbers should hold, the absolute ones are not production numbers.
  • The truth flag uses a projected angle above 4× Highland. Tail primaries with a scatter just below the threshold count as no-tail, which is why the no-tail efficiency also moves.
